Part 1: the explainer (about 90 seconds)

Press Space to start. Read one block, then press Space for the next scene.

Scene 1 · Why this is needed
Do: press Space. Start reading when the title appears.

Companies make decisions on numbers from their data, and AI now writes the SQL behind them. On BIRD, the standard benchmark, even the best systems get about one question in five wrong.

Scene 2 · The silent failure
Do: press Space.

And a wrong query does not fail. In our own test, the model searched for CANCELED in capitals. The data stores it in small letters. It answered zero. The real answer was 625.

Scene 3 · Eight attempts in a sandbox
Do: press Space.

Verdict asks the model eight times, with some randomness so the attempts differ. Each query runs in its own process: read-only, checked by the database itself, and killed if it runs too long.

Scene 4 · Group, then decide
Do: press Space. The three stamps appear one by one; match them as you speak.

We group attempts by their result, not their text, because different queries can be equally right. Six of eight agreeing is Trusted. A split, an empty answer or a zero goes to Review. If the data cannot answer, Reject.

Scene 5 · Our own model
Do: press Space.

The model is our own. We fine-tuned Qwen with LoRA, which trains a small add-on and leaves the model frozen. On 1,534 unseen questions it went from 24.6 to 43.5 percent.

Scene 6 · Reinforcement learning
Do: press Space. Do not move on until the bottom panel has appeared.

Next is reinforcement learning, with a GRPO loop we wrote by hand. Each query runs on the real database: one point for a right result, a tenth for one that only runs. Answers that beat their group's average are reinforced. In a small run the model learned to chase that tenth, so we test for it.

Scene 7 · What runs today
Do: press Space. After the last word, press Space once more: the screen wipes to black.

The finished model is a 1.6 gigabyte file that runs on a laptop with no internet, backed by 321 automated tests. Here is the real app.

Part 2: the real app (about 80 seconds)

Switch to Tab 2. Each answer takes about 9 seconds to build on screen. Talk while it builds.

Section: Ask page
Do: show the page for a second. Database says Olist e-commerce.

This is the real app. The data is Olist, a real online store with about 100,000 orders. What you will see is a recorded run from our own model, replayed.

Section: Ask page · first question
Do: paste How many orders were canceled? and press Ask. Read while the eight queries appear.

The same question from before. Verdict writes eight queries, runs each one in the sandbox, and groups the results.

Wait for the green TRUSTED card.

Trusted: 625. Six of eight attempts agree.

Section: Receipt (opens from the answer card)
Do: open the receipt. Move slowly: attempts first, then the checks. Close it when done.

This is the receipt. Here are all eight queries. Two of them added a join that was not needed and got 542. They were outvoted. These checks ran. These two say off, because they did not run.

Section: Ask page · second question
Do: paste How many orders were delivered late in 2018? and press Ask. Read while it builds.

A harder question. All eight queries run without an error, and they give seven different answers.

Wait for the red REJECT card.

Any one of these alone would look fine. Verdict shows no number and sends it to a person.

Section: Review queue (left menu)
Do: click Review queue. Open the "delivered late in 2018" item. Paste the SQL from the end of this page, click Run (it shows 4,944), then click Approve my fix.

The analyst sees only these cases. I run the right query, 4,944, and approve it. It is saved with the question for later use.

Section: Ask page · third question
Do: click Ask in the left menu. Paste What was our return on ad spend in 2018? and press Ask. Wait for the red REJECT card.

And a question the data cannot answer. There is no advertising spend in this database. Five of eight attempts said so, and Verdict rejects it without making up a number.

Section: Ask page · close
Do: stay on the Reject card. Stop recording after the last word.

Verdict gives every answer a level, shows its evidence, and says when it does not know. One honest limit: agreement is not proof. Measuring how often Trusted is wrong is our next step. Thank you.
